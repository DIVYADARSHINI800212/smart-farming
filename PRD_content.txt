1. Executive Summary
Smart Farming Assistant is an AI-powered precision agriculture platform that combines low-cost field sensor nodes, farmer-captured mobile images, and an edge AI gateway to continuously monitor crop and environmental health. The system detects early signs of disease and pest infestation, evaluates irrigation and weather-related risk, and delivers actionable, farmer-friendly recommendations through a web dashboard and mobile alerts.
The platform is designed to work in low-connectivity rural environments by processing sensor and image data locally at the edge, syncing to the cloud dashboard when connectivity is available. It targets smallholder and mid-size farmers who currently rely on manual field inspection and delayed expert consultation, both of which increase the risk of yield loss.
2. Problem Statement
Farmers often detect crop disease and pest infestations only after visible, large-scale damage has occurred, by which point yield loss is difficult to reverse.
Manual field monitoring is time-consuming, inconsistent, and dependent on the farmer's personal experience and availability.
Irrigation decisions are frequently made on intuition rather than actual soil moisture, weather, and crop water-stress data, leading to over- or under-irrigation.
Access to timely agronomic expertise (correct treatment, dosage, and application timing) is limited in rural areas, and misapplication of pesticides is common.
Existing precision-agriculture solutions are often costly, cloud-dependent, and unsuitable for regions with unreliable internet connectivity.
3. Goals and Objectives
3.1 Product Goals
Enable early, automated detection of crop disease and pest risk using a combination of sensor data and AI-based image analysis.
Provide farmers with clear, prioritized, and actionable recommendations rather than raw sensor data.
Optimize water usage through smart, data-driven irrigation recommendations.
Operate reliably in low-connectivity conditions through local edge processing.
Keep hardware and deployment costs low enough for smallholder adoption (2 field nodes + 1 edge gateway per deployment unit).

5. Scope
5.1 In Scope (SIH Prototype)
Two field sensor nodes (soil moisture, temperature, humidity, rain, optional water level) communicating over LoRa/wireless to an edge gateway.
Edge AI Gateway performing sensor data processing, image preprocessing, disease AI, pest AI, sensor+vision fusion, and local risk/decision engine.
Mobile image capture flow for farmers to photograph affected leaves/plants, tagged by zone.
Web dashboard covering: Dashboard, Field Monitoring, AI Crop Analysis, Smart Irrigation, Environment & Weather, Farmer Advisory, Analytics, Alerts, Settings.
AI-generated treatment recommendations including product, dosage, application timing, and nearby dealer availability.
Offline-first local processing with sync to cloud dashboard when internet is available.
5.2 Out of Scope (Future Phases)
Drone/satellite-based large-area imaging.
Automated pesticide/irrigation actuation (system is advisory-only in this phase).
Marketplace/e-commerce transactions with dealers (directory and contact only).
Multi-crop yield price forecasting and market-linkage features.
6. Functional Requirements — Feature Modules
The web application is organized into nine primary modules, corresponding to the dashboard information architecture below.
6.1 Dashboard (Home)
Farm health, crop health, soil moisture, temperature, and humidity summary widgets.
Pest risk, disease risk, and weather risk indicators at a glance.
Active alerts panel and AI recommendation summary.
Node status and last-updated timestamp.
6.2 Field Monitoring
Interactive field map showing zones, sensor node locations, and per-zone health/risk status.
Per-zone soil moisture, temperature, humidity, last captured image, and last update time.
6.3 AI Crop Analysis
Image capture and upload flow (camera or gallery), with preprocessing (quality check, resize, crop to region of interest, normalize).
Disease detection and pest detection results with confidence score and severity level.
Affected area/zone tagging and AI diagnosis summary.
Detection history log per zone.
6.4 Smart Irrigation
Soil moisture, soil temperature, water stress, and over-irrigation risk indicators.
Irrigation status and recommendation (target zone, recommended duration, timing) generated from sensor + weather fusion.
Rainfall forecast integration to avoid irrigating ahead of expected rain.
Water consumption tracking, water saved metric, and irrigation history.
6.5 Environment & Weather
Current conditions and multi-day forecast (temperature, humidity, rainfall, rain probability, wind).
Heat stress, drought, and flood risk indicators.
Disease-conducive condition flagging (e.g., high humidity + recent rain) and suitable spray/application weather windows.
Weather alerts feed.
6.6 Farmer Advisory
AI diagnosis and risk level combining image and sensor fusion output.
Recommended action, treatment recommendation (active ingredient, product, dosage, method, frequency, timing).
Weather suitability check before recommending application.
Product availability: nearby dealer name, distance, and contact.
Estimated product and treatment cost, safety information, pre-harvest interval, and follow-up recommendation.
6.7 Analytics
Trend charts: crop health, disease, pest activity, soil moisture, temperature, humidity, rainfall, irrigation, and water usage.
Treatment and alert history, field performance summary, yield risk factors, and historical data view.
6.8 Alerts
Consolidated, severity-ranked alert feed (critical/high/medium/low) across disease, pest, irrigation, heat, flood, drought, and weather categories.
Each alert includes location/zone, timestamp, and recommended action; full alert history retained.
6.9 Settings
Farm, field, crop, and node/sensor configuration.
Alert and notification preferences, weather API and data source configuration, language selection, and system status view.

8. Assumptions and Constraints
Each deployment unit covers a limited field area using two sensor nodes; larger farms require multiple units.
Farmers have access to a basic smartphone with a camera and local Wi-Fi connectivity to the edge gateway.
Edge AI Gateway has sufficient local compute for lightweight disease/pest CNN inference; cloud sync is best-effort, not required for core alerts.
Treatment recommendations are advisory; final application decisions and regulatory compliance remain the farmer's responsibility.
Initial AI models are trained on a limited set of common crop diseases/pests relevant to the pilot region and crop type.
